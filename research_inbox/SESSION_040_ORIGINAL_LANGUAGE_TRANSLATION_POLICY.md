# SESSION 040 — Original-Language and Translation-Variant Policy

> **Input class:** direct curator directive from Sean  
> **Scope:** project-wide methodology  
> **Status:** accepted as canonical research policy

## Curator directive

For every inscription, quotation, manuscript passage, map label, title, name, technical term, religious phrase, testimony, or other language-bearing artifact:

```text
preserve the original-language writing
        ↓
preserve script, spelling, punctuation and lineation where recoverable
        ↓
transliterate without replacing the original
        ↓
segment words and morphology
        ↓
record literal glosses
        ↓
record every materially plausible translation
        ↓
state the assumptions behind each translation
        ↓
separate later reinterpretation from translation
```

Where a source can reasonably be interpreted in more than one way, the project must not select one translation and silently discard the others.

## What counts as a materially plausible translation

A translation variant qualifies when supported by one or more of:

- grammar or syntax;
- lexical range in an appropriate historical dictionary;
- ambiguity in script, damaged text, abbreviation or word division;
- alternate manuscript, edition or map reading;
- historical semantic range;
- genre or ritual context;
- specialist scholarly translation;
- documented dispute among translators;
- proper-name versus common-noun ambiguity;
- literal versus idiomatic rendering;
- uncertain referent, tense, number, gender, case, agency or spatial relation.

The project does not preserve arbitrary paraphrases unsupported by the original text.

## Required distinctions

```text
original writing
        ≠
transliteration
        ≠
word-for-word gloss
        ≠
possible translation
        ≠
preferred contextual translation
        ≠
later doctrinal, political or conspiracy reinterpretation
```

## First motivating case

`Mahoraga / mahā-uraga` exposed two opposite collapses:

1. an older religious term was erased by a modern manga character sharing the name;
2. the loaded English gloss `Great Reptilians` projected a modern biological and conspiracy ontology backward onto a serpent term.

The correct response is not to install one new authoritative English phrase. It is to preserve the original term, morphology, attested lexical range, translation variants and later uses separately.

## Project-wide effect

The policy applies prospectively to all new acquisitions and retrospectively when older records become load-bearing.

Priority retrospective targets include:

- ancient and religious serpent terms;
- Terra Australis map labels and inscriptions;
- expedition quotations translated from non-English sources;
- occult and esoteric terminology;
- foreign-language fiction proposed as a visual or narrative precursor;
- testimony translated through investigators or journalists;
- titles whose shortening or translation changes document type;
- repeated phrases used as bridge or copied-error fingerprints.

## Canonical rule

> No ambiguous translated phrase may be used as a load-bearing bridge, lineage claim, motif match, copied fingerprint, or historical conclusion unless the original-language witness and materially plausible translation alternatives are preserved.
