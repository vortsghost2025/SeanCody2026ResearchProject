Overview
Richard S. Shaver’s “I Remember Lemuria!” cycle, as edited and promoted by Ray Palmer in Amazing Stories in the mid‑1940s, assembled most elements of a hostile technological subterranean civilization years before “flying saucers” and later underground‑base lore like Dulce entered UFO discourse. Palmer’s editorial framing oscillated between “fiction” and “true story,” creating an early template in pulp culture for material presented simultaneously as entertainment and suppressed testimony. Dulce narratives emerging from Paul Bennewitz and later writers have been explicitly compared by scholars and Palmer’s biographers to the Shaver Mystery, but documented citation paths are sparse and often inferential.

Title and Editorial Framing
Original title: “I Remember Lemuria!”—Palmer’s chosen title for the rewritten story based on Shaver’s “A Warning to Future Man.”

Editorial changes:

Palmer rewrote Shaver’s 10,000‑word explanatory letter into a 31,000‑word narrative story, adding characters, plot, and pulp adventure structure while preserving the core “facts” as Shaver presented them.

Crucially, Palmer reframed the origins as “racial memory” rather than literal physical exploration of underground caves, which he later described as his “one instance” of altering the factual basis.

Subsequent reprint credit: In The Hidden World A‑1, the story is explicitly attributed to “Ray Palmer & Richard S. Shaver,” acknowledging the editorial co‑authorship that had been uncredited in the pulp.

Key Passages (Deros, Caves, Machinery, Rays, Human Manipulation)
Because the original Amazing Stories scan is not fully accessible yet, passages below are taken from Palmer’s long editorial “Invitation to Adventure” and related Hidden World material that paraphrases and summarizes Shaver’s claims as they appeared in “I Remember Lemuria!” and subsequent cave narratives.

Existence of vast underground caverns:

Palmer describes Shaver’s core claim that “The Earth is inhabited, underground, in gigantic caves whose area is a great deal more than the surface land area, by a race of people called by him ‘abandon‑dero’…”

Ancient advanced civilization and machinery:

The Titans and Atlans created enormous artificial caverns and “wonderful machines” that are “still in working order, since they were built almost indestructible,” including vision rays, mental machines, death rays, and teleportation devices.

Deros using rays for torture and mind control:

Deros operate “marvelous vision rays that can penetrate miles of solid rock,” “mental machines which caused seemingly solid illusions, dreams, hypnotic compulsions,” and death rays that cause accidents and disasters among surface people.

Palmer explicitly links dero “dream mech” to surface nightmares and sudden homicidal impulses: “the case of the young girl who said ‘God told me to stab mother with a knife.’”

Human manipulation and abduction:

Deros kidnap surface humans “from the surface to serve as victims of their evil rituals” and regard human flesh as a delicacy, with scenes of torture including dismemberment and cooking.

Sexual debauchery and “stim machines”:

Deros use machines “for the revitalizing of sex known as ‘stim’ machines … these degenerates sometimes spend their whole lives in a sexual debauch that actually deforms their bodies in horrible ways almost beyond mentioning.”

These elements—hostile underground beings, advanced machinery, ray‑based mind control, torture, sexual exploitation, and surface abductions—constitute the “full hostile‑underground‑technological‑being package” by 1945, at least in Palmer’s mediated presentation of Shaver’s material.

Factual vs. Fictional Presentation
In Amazing Stories, “I Remember Lemuria!” was presented as a story but surrounded by editorial rhetoric that suggested it might be factual, and Palmer later emphasized that it was derived from “thought records” rather than conventional fiction.

Palmer states in Hidden World that the Shaver material was “offered partly as fact and partly in the guise of fiction” between 1943 and 1948, acknowledging deliberate ambiguity.

The June 1947 all‑Shaver issue was subtitled “The Most Sensational True Story Ever Told,” explicitly marketing the Shaver cycle as truth.

Palmer’s own editorials alternately stress scientific evaluation and admit that the material appears in a fiction magazine, blurring genre boundaries.

Provisional evidence class for SHAVER‑001:

Publication genre: pulp science‑fiction story.

Palmer framing: hybrid—marketed as “true” but published as entertainment; described as “thought records” whose “fictional” portions are explicitly distinguished in The Hidden World.

Shaver’s own attitude: appears to insist on factuality, seeing stories as warnings about real subterranean beings.

SHAVER‑002 — Deros and Teros
Canonical ID
Deros record: SHAVER‑002‑DERO

Teros record: SHAVER‑002‑TERO

Deros: Original Descriptions
Physical appearance

Deros are “midget‑like idiots” who have degenerated physically due to lack of beneficial solar rays and long exposure to radioactive contamination and “de” rays.

Shaver and Palmer describe them as deformed by perpetual use of “stim” machines and indulgence in torture and sexual debauchery.

Origin

Deros are “abandon‑dero” (abandoned detrimental robots), descendants of those left behind when the Titan/Atlan civilization evacuated Earth due to harmful solar radiation.

The term “dero” compresses “detrimental” + “ro” (compulsory governance), indicating beings governed by destructive forces.

Technology

Deros operate inherited Atlantean/Titan machinery:

Vision rays, teleportation devices, death rays, “dream mech” for projecting illusions and compulsions, “stim” sexual devices, ben rays for healing and restoring energy.

They also use space ships and giant rockets (“flying saucers”) riding in the upper atmosphere.

Underground locations

Deros inhabit “gigantic caves whose area is a great deal more than the surface land area,” carved or modified by the ancient advanced civilization.

These caverns exist beneath thick layers of rock; Shaver claims to have physically entered some caves (though he later questions the line between dream and reality).

Relationship with surface humans

Deros treat surface humans as experimental subjects and food; they kidnap people for torture and consumption.

They cause accidents, wars, and personal misfortunes by directing destructive rays and mental machines at individuals and societies.

Mind‑control / “ray” claims

Deros use mental machines to impose “hypnotic compulsions,” solid illusions, and suicidal or murderous urges among surface dwellers.

Nightmares and certain “voices” in people’s heads are attributed to dero “dream mech.”

Abduction, torture, sexual, genetic, surveillance motifs

Abduction: repeated stories of humans being taken underground from the surface.

Torture: drawn‑and‑quartered victims, boiling in oil, and sadistic rituals are central motifs.

Sexual exploitation: stim machines and perpetual orgiastic debauch shape dero culture.

Surveillance: omnipresent “vision rays” allow deros to watch surface people and subterranean opponents continuously.

Genetic modification: less explicit in early stories; degeneration arises from environment and ray exposure rather than deliberate genetic engineering.

Evidence class (publication vs. literal claim)

Shaver insists these beings are concretely physical (“the blood of a tero spurts as stickily and redly…”), rejecting spiritist interpretations.

Palmer, while emphasizing their “scientific” basis and calling for empirical proof, presents the stories in a fiction magazine, toggling between hoax skepticism and sincere advocacy.

Teros: Original Descriptions
Physical appearance

Teros are relatively healthy underground inhabitants who use beneficial rays and technology to counteract degenerative effects; described as more noble and less deformed than deros.

Origin

Teros are descendants of the same Atlantean/Titan populations but retain more of the constructive technological and ethical orientation.

The term “tero” compresses “te” (integrative/constructive) + “ro” (governed by), indicating beings governed by constructive forces.

Technology

Teros also employ ancient machines, particularly beneficial rays (ben rays) for healing and longevity, and defensive systems against deros.

Underground locations

Teros exist in scattered, hidden enclaves in the cavern systems, often outnumbered and hunted by deros.

Relationship with surface humans

Teros sometimes act as distant mentors or protectors, attempting to help surface humans via beneficial ray projection or covert assistance.

Mind‑control / “ray” claims

Teros use rays to heal or provide insight rather than to dominate, though Shaver’s narratives sometimes blur these functions.

Abduction, torture, sexual, genetic, surveillance motifs

Teros are more often depicted as resisting such practices, but they operate within the same infrastructure of surveillance and teleportation.

Evidence class

As with deros, Shaver maintains their physical reality; Palmer’s editorials treat them alternately as factual possibilities and vivid allegories.

Terminology Origins: Shaver vs. Palmer
Mantong alphabet and core concepts (Titans, Atlans, abandon‑dero, dero, tero) originate in Shaver’s letters and original manuscripts (“An Ancient Language?” and “A Warning to Future Man”).

Palmer acknowledges that he translated Shaver’s cave experiences into “racial memory” for magazine presentation but treats the terminology as Shaver’s own invention.

Scholarly commentary (e.g., Beggan) emphasizes that Palmer exercised a “heavy editorial hand,” expanding and dramatizing the material but not originating key neologisms.

SHAVER‑003 — Reader‑Response Phenomenon
Canonical ID
Record: SHAVER‑003

Volume of Reader Mail and Circulation Changes
Palmer reports that after publishing Shaver’s Mantong letter (“An Ancient Language?”) he received “many hundreds of letters,” prompting him to commission a story from Shaver.

“I Remember Lemuria!” (March 1945) produced an unprecedented response: “a flood of letters … that totaled, in the end, more than 50,000 letters,” compared to the usual 45–50 letters per month.

Beggan, summarizing Nadis, notes that Amazing Stories circulation rose from about 135,000 to 185,000 copies per issue during the Shaver Mystery period, with “I Remember Lemuria!” selling out.

Letters Claiming Personal Cave Experiences
Palmer states he possessed “thousands of letters all saying the same thing. It was true. Thousands of people were relating identical experiences, identical voices telling them identical things.”

In a May 1947 letter printed in Amazing Stories, an ex‑Marine writes, “after reading the August issue, I have come to the conclusion that Shaver is quite right… I have always believed there was a race of people living under the earth. I am an ex‑marine and would like very much to help in finding these people.”

Palmer characterizes these letters as “almost incredible confirmation from readers and others having had the same experiences Mr. Shaver has had,” though he admits they are anecdotal.

Amazing Stories Continuation of the Mystery
Between March 1945 and May 1948, about two dozen Shaver Mystery stories appeared in Amazing Stories, many as cover features.

A June 1947 all‑Shaver issue included four stories plus supporting articles (Mantong alphabet guide, Shaver’s own essays), branded “The Most Sensational True Story Ever Told.”

Reader backlash, including organized boycotts by fan groups, led Ziff‑Davis management to eventually bar Shaver stories; Palmer resigned his editorship in 1949.

Reader Participation in Mythology Expansion
Palmer explicitly invited readers to submit experiences to “tabulate” and seek concrete proof: “If you will describe it faithfully to us, the tabulated results will be presented in Amazing Stories…”

This feedback loop allowed readers’ letters to add detail and corroborative anecdotes, reinforcing and expanding the mythos (additional cave locations, torture scenarios, ray effects).

Scholars now interpret this collective response as a proto‑conspiracy‑culture phenomenon, where story, editorial framing, and audience participation co‑create a pseudo‑empirical underground mythology.
