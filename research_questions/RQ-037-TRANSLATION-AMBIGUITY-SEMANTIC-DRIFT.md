# RQ-037 — Which historical connections depend on translation ambiguity, semantic drift or translator choice?

```yaml
RQ:
  id: RQ-037
  title: "Which historical connections depend on translation ambiguity, semantic drift or translator choice?"
  status: SEARCHING
  priority: HIGH

  scope:
    - inscriptions and manuscript passages
    - map labels, legends and place-names
    - titles, quotations and repeated phrases
    - religious and cosmological terminology
    - occult, esoteric and technical vocabulary
    - translated testimony and journalism
    - names reused across languages and media
    - translator dependency and copied renderings
    - machine-translation errors
    - modern ideological or conspiracy reinterpretation

  central_problem: >
    One English rendering can hide multiple possible meanings, create false motif matches,
    erase older entities, introduce modern categories, or manufacture apparent transmission.
    The project must preserve original-language witnesses and all materially plausible
    translation variants before using wording as evidence.

  core_questions:
    - What is the exact original script and source location?
    - Is the transcription secure?
    - How can the text be segmented or parsed differently?
    - What lexical ranges were available in the historical period?
    - Which translations are literal, idiomatic, doctrinal, technical or modernizing?
    - Which English terms are loaded or anachronistic?
    - Do published translations derive from one another?
    - Does an alleged shared phrase exist in the original languages or only in translation?
    - Did a later movement adopt one translator's wording rather than the original text?
    - Which ambiguity remains unresolved after specialist comparison?

  do_not_treat_as_proof:
    - one English translation without the original text
    - dictionary gloss detached from sentence and genre context
    - etymology as the complete contextual meaning
    - identical English wording as proof of identical originals
    - different English wording as proof of different originals
    - a modern biological category projected onto an older mythic term
    - catalogue normalization as original wording
    - machine translation as sole authority
    - one translator's preference as textual certainty
    - arbitrary paraphrases unsupported by grammar or lexical evidence

  partial_resolution: >
    At least five load-bearing project terms or passages receive complete translation records
    containing original script, transliteration, segmentation, major materially plausible
    translations, translator provenance, ambiguity profile and later reinterpretations.

  positive_resolution: >
    The project identifies where translation materially changes proposed lineages, map readings,
    motif identities or copied fingerprints, and revises those claims using original-language
    comparison and explicit variant sets.

  negative_resolution: >
    A proposed ambiguity disappears after original-language inspection and one reading is strongly
    supported; alternate internet renderings are classified as mistranslation, modernization,
    paraphrase or later reinterpretation.
```

## Project-wide rule

```text
No ambiguous translated phrase may be load-bearing
without an original-language witness and variant record.
```

This applies to:

- bridge claims;
- motif matches;
- map lineage;
- copied-error fingerprints;
- quotation chains;
- titles and bylines;
- ancient-to-modern reinterpretation;
- testimony convergence.
