## 2. Critical title correction

The correct title is:

> *Remarks on a review of Symmes' theory, which appeared in the American quarterly review*

The common variant is:

> *Remarks of Symmes' Theory Which Appeared in the American Quarterly Review*

The difference matters. The correct title identifies a response to a review; the shortened variant makes the work sound like a freestanding exposition of Symmes's theory.

## 3. What the title proves

The title supports:

- Reynolds remained publicly engaged with printed discussion of Symmes's theory in 1827;
- the pamphlet belongs to a review-and-response print exchange;
- the artifact postdates both the reported 1825 personal break and Adams's November 4, 1826 `varied his purpose` description;
- Reynolds's transition was not a simple disappearance from all Symmes-related print controversy.

## 4. What the title does not prove

Until the pamphlet text is inspected, the repository must not state that Reynolds:

- defended Symmes;
- remained doctrinally loyal;
- endorsed polar openings or a habitable interior in 1827;
- rebutted every criticism in the AQR article;
- carried those claims into his later expedition program.

`Remarks on a review` could contain defense, qualification, modification, strategic distancing, or rejection. The text decides.

## 5. Chronology correction

The sequence is:

```text
reported Reynolds–Symmes break: circa 1825
        ↓
Adams says Reynolds had "varied his purpose": 1826-11-04
        ↓
Reynolds pamphlet responding to AQR review: 1827
        ↓
South-Pole lobbying identity: 1828
        ↓
fully institutional 1836 Address without explicit Symmes/hollow language
```

The 1827 artifact therefore complicates an **instantaneous clean-break** story. It does not yet overturn the 1836 negative-evidence record.
