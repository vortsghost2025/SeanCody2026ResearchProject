## Supported chronology

### November 4, 1826 — contemporary pivot description

John Quincy Adams's published diary describes Reynolds as having lectured in support of Symmes's theory and then having `varied his purpose` toward Southern Ocean circumnavigation.

This supports a same-person transition, not complete abandonment.

## Verified publication carrier

Reynolds's preface states that the three response numbers `were first published in the National Intelligencer`.

The collected pamphlet preserves No. I, No. II, and No. III and signs the responses `J. N. Reynolds`.

```text
American Quarterly Review criticism
        ↓
National Intelligencer numbered response series
        ↓
Gales & Seaton collected pamphlet
```

## 1828–1836 institutionalization

By February 1828 Adams described Reynolds as the projector of an expedition to the South Pole. By 1834 the proposal circulated through state and congressional print channels. Reynolds's 1836 *Address* foregrounded science, national honor, navigation, commerce, whaling, surveying, and knowledge.

The available 1836 OCR yields:

```text
Symmes: no match
hollow: no match
```

This supports absence of explicit doctrinal vocabulary in that publication. It does not erase the 1827 partial defense.
