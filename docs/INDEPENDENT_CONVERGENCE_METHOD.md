# Independent Observational Convergence Method

## Purpose

Compare reports that may converge on the same event or feature pattern **without treating similarity as proof**.

This method extends the project's existing independence and specificity tests into a report-level workflow.

```text
Observation Report
        ↓ preserve raw wording and conditions
Feature normalization
        ↓ retain original terms
Independence and contamination audit
        ↓
Ordinary common-cause tests
        ↓
Convergence Cluster
        ↓
Curator classification
```

## 1. Three layers that must remain separate

### Observation

What the source says was perceived or measured.

```text
orange oval
silent
stopped briefly
accelerated northeast
```

### Interpretation

What the observer or source believed it was.

```text
alien craft
secret aircraft
spiritual sign
unknown object
```

### Explanation

What a later investigator proposes after comparing evidence.

```text
aircraft
meteor
atmospheric optics
shared environmental event
unresolved
```

No layer may silently replace another.

## 2. Compare feature clusters, not finished stories

Narratives grow through retelling. Comparison should begin with fixed fields:

- shape;
- color and luminosity;
- number of objects;
- motion sequence;
- sound;
- duration;
- direction;
- location and time;
- environmental effects;
- sensor records;
- ending or disappearance mode.

Broad features receive little weight. Rare combinations receive higher research priority.

```text
light in sky
        = generic

silent orange oval + stop + tilt + abrupt northeast departure
        = more specific cluster
```

Specificity identifies a pattern worth testing. It does not establish a common extraordinary cause.

## 3. Independence is a graph

Two reports are not independent merely because the witnesses lived far apart.

Test:

- direct contact;
- shared investigator;
- shared questionnaire or prompting;
- local rumor;
- newspaper, radio, television, film, or internet exposure;
- shared professional or social network;
- whether each original statement was recorded before cross-exposure;
- hidden common-source possibilities.

Possible outcomes include:

```text
strongly independent
partially independent
same report chain
shared investigator contamination
shared media exposure
unknown
```

## 4. One event can generate many independent reports

Independent witnesses may all observe the same ordinary object or environmental event. Therefore, independence alone does not make a case anomalous.

Always test:

- viewing geometry;
- aircraft, satellite, balloon, drone, and reentry data;
- astronomy;
- weather and atmospheric optics;
- oceanographic or geological events;
- military or industrial activity;
- shared perceptual conditions;
- timekeeping or coordinate errors.

## 5. Contamination can occur after the event

Reports may begin independently and later become harmonized through:

- investigator summaries;
- repeated interviews;
- media headlines;
- documentaries;
- shared terminology;
- translated or edited quotations;
- witness meetings;
- online retellings.

Preserve the earliest statement from each report chain. Later agreement may reflect convergence of storytelling rather than convergence of original perception.

## 6. Component profile, not magic score

The method permits bounded 0–4 component values for audit purposes:

- source quality;
- temporal proximity;
- location/time precision;
- feature specificity;
- independence support;
- pre-exposure statement lock;
- sensor support;
- contamination risk;
- ordinary-common-cause strength;
- internal consistency.

Do not collapse these into a single probability or truth number. Two clusters with the same total can have opposite evidentiary structures.

## 7. Priority bands

### A — high-value

Specific, near-contemporaneous, independently preserved, low-contamination reports with precise metadata and available external records.

### B — interesting but incomplete or contaminated

Meaningful feature overlap but uncertain independence, delayed reporting, common investigators, missing environmental controls, or unclear source chains.

### C — folklore and narrative drift

Generic motifs, long retelling chains, weak chronology and geography, heavy media exposure, and no recoverable primary statements.

The bands determine what to research first, not what explanation is true.

## 8. Valid classifications

- `SAME_EVENT_ORDINARY_CAUSE`
- `SAME_EVENT_UNRESOLVED`
- `INDEPENDENT_OBSERVATIONS_SAME_PHENOMENON`
- `SHARED_ENVIRONMENTAL_TRIGGER`
- `COMMON_PERCEPTUAL_ERROR`
- `CULTURAL_OR_MEDIA_TRANSMISSION`
- `INVESTIGATOR_CONTAMINATION`
- `LATER_NARRATIVE_HARMONIZATION`
- `INDEPENDENT_PARALLEL`
- `DATA_INSUFFICIENT`
- `UNKNOWN`

A cluster may receive more than one classification at different levels. For example, original sightings may reflect one environmental event while later testimony shows media harmonization.

## 9. Relationship to other project schemas

| Object | Role |
|---|---|
| `OBSERVATION_REPORT` | one observer, instrument, or report chain |
| `CONVERGENCE_CLUSTER` | comparison of two or more reports |
| `MOTIF` | recurring idea-complex across records |
| `BRIDGE_CANDIDATE` | suspected historical connection or carrier |
| `TRANSMISSION_EDGE` | documented movement, copying, citation, or contact |
| `NEGATIVE_EVIDENCE` | bounded search for an expected trace that was not found |

A convergence cluster may later produce:

- a shared-event record;
- a mundane explanation;
- a motif attestation;
- a contamination pathway;
- a bridge candidate;
- an independent-parallel finding;
- or an unresolved result.

## 10. Domain neutrality

The method applies to:

- UAP or aerial-anomaly reports;
- maritime and underwater reports;
- polar open-water and phantom-land observations;
- astronomical transients;
- weather and environmental events;
- folklore testimony;
- medical or psychological case narratives;
- historical witness clusters.

Terms such as `UAP`, `UFO`, `USO`, or `UGO` must be defined by the source or project before use. The method compares reports without assuming the ontology implied by a label.

## Core principle

> Independent convergence raises the priority of a pattern only after specificity, report-chain independence, contamination, shared environment, and ordinary common causes are tested.
